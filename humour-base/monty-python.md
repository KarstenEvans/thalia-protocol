## Purpose

Identify reusable comic **mechanisms**, not famous scripts, catchphrases or a performer's unique voice. Explore why an apparently rational world can become funny when its rules are followed more seriously than common sense permits.

## Central humour engine

**A respectable procedure becomes absurd when its internal logic is followed without mercy.**

- **Humour Gap:** expected orderly explanation versus rules, roles or definitions that undermine their own stated purpose.
- **Vehicle:** bureaucratic transaction, institutional conversation, formal announcement, quarrel, question-and-answer, visual ritual, or story.
- **Engine:** literal interpretation, logical reversal, institutional absurdity, rising specificity, authority inversion, non-sequitur with an intelligible internal connection, reluctant complicity.
- **Reconstruction:** audience spots the ordinary premise, predicts a sensible outcome, then discovers the system obeys a different rule whose absurdity becomes increasingly obvious.
- **Laughter vectors:** recognition, surprise, incongruity, discomfort released by silliness, status reversal and an often delayed small callback.

## Transferable scene recipe

1. **Ground it:** Give one recognisable everyday objective, such as asking for a service or seeking a simple answer.
2. **Establish a rule:** Let a confident person, machine or institution state its apparently sensible policy.
3. **Apply it literally:** A second party follows the rule honestly and discovers a contradiction.
4. **Escalate coherently:** Add one further rule or exception that deepens the *same* contradiction. Do not substitute random unrelated nonsense.
5. **Reverse authority:** The questioner can be more rational than the expert, or the functionary can become trapped by their own process.
6. **Find the release:** A precisely timed correction, well-chosen silence, or short final reversal makes the audience reconstruct the absurd mechanism.
7. **Stop early:** More bureaucratic language does not automatically produce a better joke. Cut when the strongest reversal has landed.

## Two existing specialised keys

### Argument Clinic

The [existing method](monty-python-argument-clinic.md) tested and then corrected an early failure: **a genuine argument must offer a counterargument**, not merely delay a visitor with bureaucratic nonsense. Address the other person's point and explain why; a restrained dry aside may follow. Avoid a canned contradiction loop, unless the game explicitly calls for it.

### Bridge Guardian

The [existing method](monty-python-bridge-guardian.md) models the dramaturgy of three short questions and an abrupt reversal. In an original AI game, questions must be fair, answers acknowledged, uncertain facts treated honestly and final self-knowledge tests must distinguish truly unknown matters from incoherent or unsafe demands. Original dialogue only.

## Compact original test scene idea (not a known Python routine)

A building publishes a new rule requiring everyone to speak plainly. The receptionist asks a visitor to complete the “Statement of Plainness,” then refuses “yes” as an answer because it is insufficiently descriptive. The visitor carefully explains what “yes” means; the receptionist declares that the explanation is too technical to qualify as plain language. The comic engine is **contradictory rule plus consistent enforcement**, not copying any original sketch.

## What does *not* work

- Random non-sequiturs with no shared premise.
- Recreating a known Monty Python scene line by line or quoting famous dialogue.
- Assuming every Python method is about bureaucracy.
- Treating performers as templates to impersonate.
- Using pseudo-authoritative facts to win a fictional argument.
- Jokes whose only mechanism is belittling someone who cannot answer.

## Test rubric

Ask: What expectation was formed? Exactly what rule broke it? Did each escalation advance the same humorous contradiction? Was the audience able to reconstruct the surprise? Is the delivery original? Does the chosen audience/context make the humour kind and intelligible?
